# Evidence note

Keep an adjacent `story-evidence.md` with the brief, intended audience and source IDs. A source locator helps a person find an approved passage; it is not permission for the tool to fetch that location.

For each consequential statement use a compact row:

| Claim | Status | Source and locator | Date or window | Limit | Permitted audience |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A short, inspectable statement | observed / reported / interpreted / proposed / unknown | Supplied source ID and exact passage or position | When the observation applies | What it does not establish | Public, chosen group, or withheld |

Record unavailable or rejected claims too, so an attractive unsupported sentence does not reappear during revision. Separate a speaker's recollection from a measured observation. A design proposal is not evidence of a completed outcome, and the order of separate files does not establish the order of events.

Use only information permitted in the talk's intended setting. Do not quote or paraphrase withheld material. A private source can sometimes support an internal review without being shown in a public slide; the person must choose that boundary. Avoid embedding private paths, access tokens, hidden notes or contact details in the distributable HTML.

Read numbers with their units, denominator, dates and collection method. Voluntary feedback is not a representative outcome measure. Counts of visits, drafts or commits are not unique people, quality gains, saved time or return on investment. Do not fill a missing baseline with a plausible estimate.

When the source pack does not answer the audience's question, make the limit visible and ask for the relevant evidence or narrow the talk. This note is a review aid, not an automated truth or privacy certification.
